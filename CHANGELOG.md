## Results revised after external validation

External validation against published pharmacokinetic data found two
structural defects in the simulator (inert organ sparing; toxicant potencies on
an arbitrary scale) and a threefold-too-severe absorption penalty for
lumefantrine. After correcting and recalibrating them, several earlier claims
changed:

- **Architecture vs supervision.** Earlier versions reported the mechanistic
  solver as worth 2.5× in log-RMSE and treated architecture as the dominant
  effect. Against the corrected simulator, the matched comparison gives 1.54×,
  and auxiliary supervision is worth more (3.7×). Softening the absorption
  model during calibration made bioavailability and clearance harder to
  separate from concentrations alone, so the seven-parameter bottleneck became
  partly unidentifiable without parameter supervision.
- **Permutation importance.** An earlier version described the importance
  ranking as "exactly the causal ordering the simulator encodes". That was an
  overstatement: sex ranks third, which `scripts/diagnose_sex.py` shows is the
  model over-attributing. Water cadmium, previously ranked fourth, now falls
  outside the top six.
- **Gentamicin over-exposure.** An intermediate version reported over-exposure
  as an artefact of the uncalibrated simulator. That was premature: it had been
  suppressed by an unsourced trough threshold of 2.0 mg/L, four times more
  lenient than the published paediatric target of 0.5 mg/L (Hollander et al.
  2023). With the sourced threshold, label dosing over-exposes 12.5% of
  children and model-guided dosing 0%.
